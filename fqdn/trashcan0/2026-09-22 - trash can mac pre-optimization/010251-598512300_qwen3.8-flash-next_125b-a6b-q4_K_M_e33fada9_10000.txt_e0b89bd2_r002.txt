## Summary

Mara wanders through an old town at a leisurely pace. Along the way, she passes a bakery, listens to a clock ticking, stops for tea, watches clouds drift over a square, and remembers a rainy train ride from long ago. A traveler mentions a distant bridge, prompting her to recall a letter she once meant to send. At the market, a mapmaker describes a winding road beyond an orchard, and Mara asks where it leads. By evening, she returns along a different lane, taking in the lamps, bicycles, and the scent of fresh bread. (The story is written in a heavily repetitive, looping structure, cycling through these same vignettes many times over, as though Mara's unhurried day is a set of small, recurring impressions rather than a linear narrative.)
